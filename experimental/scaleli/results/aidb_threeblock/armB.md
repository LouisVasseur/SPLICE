# Arm B: single pass with G, T and V (20 samples, k in {1, 4, 16, 64})

**Verdict: G does not help anywhere once its table is charged.** This holds for all 80 sample-and-k cells, under every accounting that charges the gap table: `total_uncharged`, `total_charged_pwl` and `total_charged`. That covers the eight fixed orders and the priced pool. G does lower model probes on its own in 35 of 80 cells. The largest drop is osm-uniform at k=64: 8.181 down to 4.670. That is 3.51 probes saved against a 7-probe table. The closest any cell comes to breaking even is osm-uniform at k=1, which still loses by 0.75 probes (0.251 saved against a 1-probe table). Every other cell loses by more than 0.98.

## Setup
- `threeblock.py` is imported unchanged. Nothing under `experimental/scaleli` was written.
- With G off, the code reproduces `joint_results.json` with a difference of 0.0e+00 on 40 cells (baseline, T = flow_only, V = csv_only@4x, T->V = sequential@4x, 10 uniform datasets).
- Each fixed order is one pass of the blocks: `G = apply_G(k)` (select="current"), `T = apply_T`, `V = apply_V(alpha=4, guard=True)`.
- **Simultaneous priced pool (`armB.py: run_pool`).** One greedy runs over every candidate at once. The candidates are:
  - one virtual fence in any gap: costs 0 probes, counts against the int(4n) budget, ratio = +inf when it helps;
  - shrinking any gap i with W_i > W_med (same rule as the G block): costs ceil(log2(k+1))/k, the gap's amortised share;
  - fitting T once to the current slot targets in the current G coordinate: costs 6.9 (`pool`), or 0.45 in the `_pwl` variant.
  Each candidate's reduction in the objective (model_probes + gaps * share + T cost) is measured exactly on the fit_root probe metric, then divided by its own cost. The greedy takes the best ratio and stops when no candidate reduces the objective. The reported rows are re-scored with `threeblock.score`, which charges the true ceil(log2(k_eff+1)). An assertion checks that the fast evaluator equals `tb.score` to 1e-12 on all 320 pool rows. `pool` starts empty. `poolV` starts from the CSV (V-only) slots.
- Cell format in the table: `model_probes/total_charged`, plus `gN` = gaps actually shrunk (k_eff) and `vN` = virtual fences. `gap_table_probes` = ceil(log2(k_eff+1)). `total_uncharged` = model + gap table. All fields for every variant, including `total_uncharged` and `total_charged_pwl`, are in `armB.json`.

## Answers
1. **Does G help once charged? No, in 0 of 80 cells under each charged accounting.** Under `total_charged` the fixed-order winner is V (CSV alone) in 80/80 cells. Under `total_uncharged` it is T->V in 40 and V in 40. Adding the pool, the overall `total_charged` winners are poolV 64, pool 8, V 8. None of these winners uses G.
2. **The pool almost never buys a gap.** At the exact single-gap price, one shrunk gap saves at most about 0.01 model probes. The best stalled G candidate's net change is -0.99 at k=1 and -0.10 at k=64, even at the generous amortised share of 0.109. G was bought in only 2 of 160 non-pwl pool runs: planet-uniform poolV at k=16 (1 gap) and k=64 (2 gaps). In both, the true table charge makes the result worse than not buying: 5.243 becomes 5.864 and 6.699. The 6.9 T was never bought. The 0.45 T was bought in 12 of 160 pwl runs (books-uniform and planet-uniform). A one-at-a-time greedy cannot see the joint effect of 16 to 64 gaps. The fixed G->...->V orders measure that joint effect directly, and they lose too.
3. **Which order wins.** On charged totals, G->V beats the other G orders in 80/80 cells, simply because it carries no T charge. On model probes alone, the winning G order is G->T->V in 36 cells, G->V in 27 and T->G->V in 17. The winner alternates with k even within one sample (osm-uniform: G->T->V at k=4 and 64, T->G->V at k=1 and 16). The median spread among the three G orders is 0.05 probes. Of the 32 uniform cells with a known chaos band, the spread exceeds the band in only 7: books k=64 (0.64 vs 0.36), history k=4 (0.077 vs 0.050) and fb k=1-64 (0.002 vs 0.000; fb's band is 0.058 under the c4 perturbation family). **There is no consistent coarse-to-fine versus transform-first ordering effect.**
4. **G vs T as straighteners (planet-uniform).** G->V at k=64 reaches model 3.760 at a 7-probe table cost. T->V reaches 2.836 at a 6.9 transform charge, or 0.45 as a piecewise-linear table. T is the cheaper straightener there. On osm-uniform, the only gap-dominated sample, G does straighten the root: 8.18 down to 4.67. But even k=1 (cost 1) only buys 0.25.
5. **A no-G side result: the probe-direct V polish.** `poolV` takes the CSV slots and adds virtual fences that directly reduce the probe metric. It lowers model probes on 18/20 samples. books-uniform drops by 0.368 (2.728 to 2.359). That is at the edge of books' chaos band (0.361, or 0.435 under the c4 family). Elsewhere the drop is 0.002 to 0.174. osm and planet uniform are budget-bound at 1956 fences, so they are unchanged. This greedy optimises the very probe keys it is scored on, so it is in-sample by construction. Started from empty (`pool`), the same probe-direct greedy is far worse than CSV on the gap-heavy samples (osm-uniform 11.58 with 8 fences vs 8.18; books-uniform 9.67 vs 2.73), because single fences rarely change the probe count. The SSE-driven CSV greedy is the right way to start V.
6. **G-T coupling, measured.** The gap sets chosen before and after T (G vs T->G) share 12/64 gaps on books-uniform at k=64 and 25/64 on planet-uniform. On wise-uniform and covid-uniform at k=1 they share none. So T does change which gaps are largest. That coupling does not turn into a charged gain anywhere.

## Caveats
- The pool uses the amortised G price that the brief specifies. Final rows use the true ceil(log2(k_eff+1)), so the two G purchases on planet-uniform look profitable inside the pool but lose when re-scored.
- The pool fits T at most once and never refits it after a G or V step. Its V moves optimise probes, not the CSV SSE. Pool and CSV V are therefore different greedies.
- Chaos bands were not re-measured per G state. The order comparisons use last week's bands, which come from mixed perturbation families (see the harness notes).
- Window samples have no last-week reference. Their T and V numbers are new and were not regression-checked.
- The fixed-order parts were produced in an earlier run of the same `armB.py`. Six of them were recomputed in this session and matched bit for bit. All 40 pool parts were rerun in this session. 16 earlier pool parts survived from the first run, and they match the rerun exactly (the only change is two added diagnostic fields).
